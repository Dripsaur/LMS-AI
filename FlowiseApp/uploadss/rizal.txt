José Protasio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda (June 19, 1861 – December 30, 1896) is the national hero of the Philippines, a brilliant polymath, physician, and writer whose peaceful reform advocacy and powerful novels ignited the Philippine Revolution.

Early Life and Education

• Birth: Born in Calamba, Laguna, on June 19, 1861, as the seventh of eleven children in a prosperous family.
• First Teacher: His mother, Teodora Alonso, taught him how to read at a young age.
• Studies: He studied at Ateneo Municipal de Manila, the University of Santo Tomas, and later traveled to Europe to earn degrees in medicine and philosophy from the Universidad Complutense de Madrid and universities in Paris and Heidelberg.
• Polymath: He mastered over 20 languages and excelled as a writer, sculptor, ophthalmologist, and scientist.

Writings and Advocacy

• Propaganda Movement: In Europe, he joined reformists demanding equal rights, freedom of speech, and representation for the Philippines as a Spanish province—not violent separation.
• Noli Me Tángere (1887): A groundbreaking satirical novel exposing the corruption and abuses of the Spanish colonial government and the Catholic clergy.
• El Filibusterismo (1891): The darker sequel to Noli Me Tángere that further fueled nationalist sentiments.
• La Liga Filipina (1892): A civic organization he founded upon returning to Manila to unite Filipinos and promote mutual aid, which led to his arrest and exile.

Exile, Execution, and Legacy

• Exile in Dapitan: He was banished to Dapitan in northwestern Mindanao for four years, where he built a school, practiced medicine, and conducted agricultural projects.
• Trial and Execution: When the Philippine Revolution broke out in 1896 led by the Katipunan, Rizal was arrested, falsely charged with rebellion and sedition, and sentenced to death by a Spanish military court.
• Death: On December 30, 1896, at age 35, he was executed by a firing squad at Bagumbayan (now Rizal Park) in Manila.
• Last Farewell: On the eve of his execution, he penned Mi último adiós (My Last Farewell), a timeless poem of love for his homeland. His martyrdom sealed the resolve of Filipinos to fight for full independence.
